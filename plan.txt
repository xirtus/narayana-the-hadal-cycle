# NARAYANA: THE HADAL CYCLE

## MASTER DIRECTIVE

Build a complete, visually spectacular Three.js/WebGPU third-person underwater action-adventure called **NARAYANA: THE HADAL CYCLE**.

The player is a small realistic cephalopod avatar of Narayana who begins on the peak of a tiny volcanic island in the Pacific and descends continuously through the ocean, layer by layer, until reaching the floor of the Mariana Trench.

The experience should combine:

* The grace, beauty, scale and meditative wonder of **ABZÛ**
* The animal movement, sonar, mystery and alien oceanic feeling of **Ecco the Dolphin**
* The exploration, ecology, terror, discovery and depth progression of **Subnautica**
* The creature growth and predatory progression of games like **Maneater**
* The environmental mystery and almost wordless storytelling of **Journey**
* Real deep-sea biology, geology and oceanography
* Vedic cosmology, especially Narayana, Vishnu, Shiva, Varuna, Shesha, cosmic cycles, reincarnation, preservation and dissolution
* Procedural ocean ecosystems and partially procedural level generation
* Extremely responsive cephalopod locomotion where the tentacles are central to physics, movement, combat and interaction

This must feel like an actual finished game, not a tech demo.

The ocean should initially appear believable and naturalistic. The supernatural should emerge gradually as the player descends.

At 100 meters, this seems like Earth.

At 1,000 meters, something feels strange.

At 4,000 meters, ancient structures begin appearing.

At 8,000 meters, biology and mythology are becoming inseparable.

At the bottom of the Mariana Trench, physical reality has almost completely broken open.

There are lava fissures, hydrothermal cathedrals, impossible creatures, ancient machinery, Vedic geometry, Nāga structures and enormous submerged star gates leading somewhere outside ordinary space and time.

---

# 1. CENTRAL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The squid is the game.

Do not build a generic swimming controller and attach tentacle meshes to it.

The player should constantly feel like they are inhabiting an actual cephalopod.

The mantle, fins, arms, tentacles, suckers, chromatophores and jet propulsion must all participate in movement and gameplay.

Every major system should ask:

**How would a supernatural squid solve this?**

Do not give the squid a human inventory, sword, backpack or generic RPG abilities when its biology can provide something more interesting.

Its body is its toolset.

Its body is its inventory.

Its body is its interface with the world.

---

# 2. THE COSMIC STORY

The mythological conflict is not a simplistic good-god-versus-evil-god story.

Vishnu represents preservation, continuity, memory, order and the maintenance of worlds.

Shiva represents transformation, dissolution, destruction of obsolete forms and the clearing of space for creation.

Both are necessary.

The eternal spiritual conflict exists because reality continually oscillates between these principles.

Deep beneath the Pacific, something has disturbed that equilibrium.

Ancient Asuric intelligences trapped outside ordinary time have discovered the deepest physical point on Earth and are attempting to use it as an anchor.

They are turning the cosmic cycle into a machine.

Instead of creation, preservation, dissolution and rebirth, they want one permanent reality constructed entirely according to their own image.

The Mariana Trench has become a wound through which multiple ages and worlds are touching.

### Opening

Begin above water.

A tiny black volcanic island protrudes from an impossibly large Pacific Ocean.

Storm clouds gather.

The player controls a tiny juvenile squid trapped in a volcanic tide pool close to the summit.

Through environmental events and a brief cinematic, we see a mythic vision of Vishnu reclining upon Shesha on the cosmic ocean.

Varuna, the intelligence of the waters, calls downward through the physical ocean.

The little squid enters the sea.

The player initially has no idea whether the vision was literal, psychic or symbolic.

As the journey continues, it becomes apparent that the squid itself is an emanation, avatar or dream of Narayana.

Meanwhile Shiva's presence manifests through earthquakes, lava, extinctions, collapsing ruins and the cosmic rhythm of Tandava.

The player gradually discovers that Shiva is not necessarily the enemy.

The real question is whether the existing world should be preserved, transformed or allowed to end.

---

# 3. REINCARNATION

Death is canonical.

Do not simply display GAME OVER.

When the squid dies:

1. The screen dissolves into clouds of chromatophore patterns.
2. The player's consciousness travels through an abstract cosmic ocean.
3. A new juvenile incarnation emerges from the most recently activated sanctuary.
4. The procedural configuration of nearby tunnels, prey, predators and events partially changes.
5. Important spiritual knowledge remains.

This makes death part of the mythology and game mechanics.

Permanent progression represents **samskara**, impressions carried between incarnations.

Temporary biological growth belongs only to the current incarnation.

This creates a light roguelite structure without turning the game into a conventional roguelike.

---

# 4. THE SQUID MUST ACTUALLY MOVE LIKE A CEPHALOPOD

This is the single most important technical feature.

## Never do this

Do not move a capsule rigidbody around and simply animate eight tentacles behind it.

## Instead

Calculate movement as forces created by the squid's body.

### Mantle jet

The squid pulls water into its mantle and explosively expels it.

Use this for:

* Burst acceleration
* Escaping predators
* Dodging
* Breaking fragile structures
* Launching into currents
* Breaching the surface
* Rapid reverse movement

The mantle visibly expands before firing.

A strong jet folds the arms backward around the body because of hydrodynamic drag.

Jet propulsion consumes short-term muscular stamina.

### Fin locomotion

For calm cruising, mantle fins create continuous low-energy propulsion.

This is the default exploration movement.

Movement should look serene rather than constantly frantic.

### Tentacle swimming

When accelerating without jetting, arms rhythmically contract, extend and streamline.

Individual arms contribute small forces to the body.

The player body therefore reacts naturally to their movement.

### Surface crawling

Near rock, coral, wreckage or ruins, the squid can stop swimming and **walk using its arms**.

Tentacles detect surfaces.

Suckers establish contact points.

Arms pull the mantle toward those anchors.

The squid can crawl:

* Across rocks
* Under ledges
* Along ceilings
* Through ruins
* Across vertical trench walls
* Inside shipwrecks
* Through coral forests

This should feel completely different from swimming.

### Grappling

Any sufficiently strong surface can be grabbed.

One tentacle can anchor while the squid swings.

Multiple arms can establish several anchors simultaneously.

The player can:

* Grapple a ceiling
* Pull around a corner
* Swing through caves
* Catch themselves during currents
* Grab moving creatures
* Attach to whales
* Ride sharks
* Pull open doors
* Tear plants apart
* Drag objects
* Hold prey
* Brace against pressure currents

Predators sometimes detect high-energy jet propulsion.

In dangerous areas, silent tentacle traversal can therefore be safer.

### Body orientation

The squid's body orientation should result from:

* Tentacle forces
* Jet direction
* Fin steering
* Water velocity
* Collision forces
* Player steering
* Buoyancy
* Drag

The creature should feel physical rather than like a spaceship.

---

# 5. PROCEDURAL TENTACLE SYSTEM

Each major arm needs:

* Root joint
* Procedural spline
* Multiple simulated control points
* IK target
* Contact detection
* Grip state
* Sucker contact points
* Local muscle tension
* Drag
* Curl
* Relaxation
* Collision avoidance

Use an optimized procedural solution rather than giving every sucker a rigidbody.

Build several levels of simulation.

### High importance arms

The nearest 2 to 4 arms receive detailed physics and IK.

### Secondary arms

Use simplified procedural dynamics.

### Distant animation

Use spline animation and shader deformation.

Tentacles should automatically search for nearby surfaces when crawling.

They should not constantly clip through geometry.

When swimming, their configuration should respond to acceleration.

When threatened, they should bunch together.

When feeding, several arms should independently manipulate prey.

When investigating something, one or two arms may reach toward it before the player explicitly interacts.

This gives the animal visible intelligence.

---

# 6. CHROMATOPHORES

The skin of the squid is an entire gameplay system.

The player can dynamically alter:

* Color
* Contrast
* Pattern
* Brightness
* Pulsation
* Bioluminescence
* Texture frequency

Chromatophores serve several purposes.

### Camouflage

Match nearby rock, sand, coral or vegetation.

Predator detection probability decreases.

### Hunting

Approach prey while visually blending into the environment.

### Communication

Certain species understand particular color patterns.

Instead of dialogue menus, the player learns patterns.

Examples:

* Calm
* Threat
* Follow
* Food
* Warning
* Courtship
* Submission
* Curiosity

Eventually supernatural creatures communicate through extremely elaborate moving mandalas across their skin.

### Spiritual language

Ancient gates respond to specific chromatophore geometries.

The squid's own skin eventually becomes a living yantra.

---

# 7. INK

Ink must be more than a smoke grenade.

Different upgrades allow ink to become:

### Defensive ink

Creates a large visual obstruction.

### Decoy ink

Forms a temporary squid-shaped pseudo-creature.

### Predator ink

Contains chemicals attracting other predators to the target.

### Bioluminescent ink

Illuminates caverns and marks paths.

### Conductive ink

Allows interactions with electrical creatures and machinery.

### Ritual ink

At deep levels, creates temporary geometric glyphs used to activate Vedic machinery and star gates.

Ink should interact with currents and visibly deform through the water volume.

---

# 8. SONAR AND PERCEPTION

Borrow the strongest conceptual idea from Ecco without simply copying it.

The squid can release a **Narayana Pulse**.

Initially this behaves like biological sonar and pressure perception.

A wave expands outward through the environment.

For several seconds the player sees:

* Terrain silhouettes
* Moving creatures
* Hollow caves
* Weak rock
* Ancient mechanisms
* Nearby heartbeats
* Current directions

At enormous depth, sonar evolves into something stranger.

The pulse begins detecting objects that are not entirely located in the current world.

You might see a gigantic serpent wrapped around a mountain that disappears when ordinary vision returns.

---

# 9. CORE PLAYER LOOP

The rhythm should be:

**Explore -> Observe -> Hunt or evade -> Solve -> Grow -> Discover -> Descend -> Transform**

Exploration must alternate between tranquility and terror.

Do not fill every area with combat.

Sometimes the most powerful moment should be seeing an enormous whale pass overhead.

Sometimes hundreds of manta rays should migrate through a shaft.

Sometimes absolutely nothing should be visible beneath you for several hundred meters.

The player should occasionally choose to descend into apparently infinite blackness without knowing what is waiting below.

---

# 10. ORGANIC PROGRESSION

Avoid conventional weapons and armor.

Progression changes the animal.

Three interconnected evolutionary paths correspond loosely to cosmic principles.

## Narayana / Varuna

Water, perception and adaptation.

Unlock:

* Better sonar
* Stronger swimming
* Current sensing
* Chromatophore mastery
* Improved grappling
* Pressure adaptation
* Symbiosis
* Bioluminescence

## Vishnu

Preservation and protection.

Unlock:

* Regeneration
* Defensive chromatophore shell
* Improved camouflage
* Protective ink
* Creature pacification
* Healing symbiosis
* Temporary stabilization of collapsing environments

## Shiva

Transformation and destruction.

Unlock:

* Violent mantle jets
* Tentacle strikes
* Grapple tearing
* Pressure shockwaves
* Armor breaking
* Predator intimidation
* Thermal resistance
* Ability to destroy corrupted structures

The most powerful abilities require combinations instead of forcing the player into only one tree.

---

# 11. SIZE PROGRESSION

The squid physically grows.

## Hatchling

Tiny, vulnerable and extremely agile.

Can enter holes larger creatures cannot.

Primary gameplay:

* Hiding
* Exploring tide pools
* Eating plankton and tiny crustaceans
* Learning chromatophores

## Juvenile

Unlock:

* Ink
* Basic grappling
* Hunting small fish
* More powerful jet
* Basic sonar

## Adult

Unlock:

* Large prey
* Multiple simultaneous tentacle anchors
* Environmental manipulation
* Combat against medium predators
* Riding larger animals

## Giant / Divine Squid

Late game.

The squid becomes an enormous abyssal cephalopod.

Small creatures now form schools around the player.

The player can grapple leviathans.

Ancient doors that once appeared gigantic can now be physically moved.

Scale should fundamentally change gameplay rather than merely increasing statistics.

# 12. WORLD STRUCTURE

Build the ocean as a giant vertically streamed world.

The complete descent should feel approximately continuous.

## PROLOGUE: THE ISLAND

Altitude to sea level.

A tiny volcanic mountain rises from the ocean.

Features:

* Tide pools
* Crabs
* Seabirds
* Rain
* Waves
* Waterfalls
* Black volcanic rock
* Tiny caves
* First vision of Vishnu and Shesha

Teach crawling before teaching swimming.

The squid reaches a waterfall and is carried down into the Pacific.

Then the world opens.

---

## ZONE I: SUNLIGHT KINGDOM

0 to 200 meters.

Beautiful tropical water.

Coral reefs.

Sea turtles.

Dolphins.

Manta rays.

Sharks.

Huge schools of fish.

Kelp.

Shipwrecks.

Whale songs.

The game initially feels peaceful.

Introduce:

* Swimming
* Hunting
* Chromatophores
* Basic sonar
* Riding large animals
* Environmental puzzles

The player repeatedly sees an impossibly deep blue canyon in the distance.

---

## ZONE II: TWILIGHT DESCENT

200 to 1,000 meters.

Sunlight becomes blue and dim.

Bioluminescence begins.

Vertical cliffs dominate the geography.

Creatures include:

* Lanternfish
* Squid
* Jellyfish
* Siphonophores
* Oarfish
* Hatchetfish
* Predatory sharks

Human infrastructure appears:

* Research cables
* Autonomous submarines
* Sonar stations
* Lost ROVs
* Secret listening arrays

The first architecture that cannot be explained as human appears embedded in the trench wall.

---

## ZONE III: MIDNIGHT OCEAN

1,000 to 4,000 meters.

Almost total darkness.

This area should emphasize fear of scale.

Large regions have almost no terrain.

The player descends through black open ocean.

The only references for distance may be falling organic particles and distant bioluminescence.

Occasional enormous creatures emerge from darkness.

Giant squid encounters begin.

Sperm whales dive through parts of the environment.

Ancient geometric structures appear.

Some appear older than human civilization.

---

## ZONE IV: ABYSSAL KINGDOM

4,000 to 6,000 meters.

The player reaches vast abyssal plains.

Features:

* Sea cucumbers
* Tripod fish
* Dumbo octopus
* Glass sponges
* Xenophyophores
* Whale falls
* Brine pools
* Methane seeps

Entire ecosystems gather around dead leviathans.

Ruins become significant.

Stone architecture seems simultaneously natural and artificial.

Large Nāga motifs begin appearing.

The player discovers that the structures may not have been built in ordinary chronological order.

---

## ZONE V: THE HADAL GATES

6,000 to 8,000 meters.

The trench narrows.

Massive vertical walls create underwater canyons.

Earthquakes occur.

Currents become violent.

Predators have exaggerated pressure-adapted anatomy.

The environment mixes genuine hadal biology with increasingly impossible creatures.

Introduce:

* Pressure storms
* Collapsing caverns
* Lava intrusions
* Superheated water
* Giant amphipods
* Albino predators
* Ancient guardians

Shiva's presence becomes obvious.

Every earthquake resembles a beat in an impossibly slow cosmic dance.

---

## ZONE VI: MARIANA LABYRINTH

8,000 to 10,500 meters.

Reality begins fragmenting.

Normal terrain exists alongside structures that seem to occupy more dimensions than they should.

Possible encounters:

* Nāga leviathans
* Crystal jellyfish
* Blind dragonfish swarms
* Giant translucent cephalopods
* Living mandalas
* Stone creatures
* Asuric biological machines

Some passages change after reincarnation.

Some exist only when viewed using sonar.

Others can only be entered while displaying the correct chromatophore pattern.

---

## ZONE VII: CHALLENGER DEEP

Approximately 10,500 to 11,000 meters.

The biological and mythological worlds merge completely.

Hydrothermal vents tower like temples.

Molten rock illuminates fissures.

Huge fields of tube worms resemble forests.

The ocean floor contains enormous circular machines.

At first they resemble ruins.

Eventually the player realizes they are **star gates**.

They predate the surrounding geology.

Shesha-like creatures coil around them.

The largest structure is buried underneath the trench floor.

The entire Mariana Trench may actually have formed around it.

---

## ZONE VIII: THE COSMIC OCEAN

Endgame.

The deepest gate opens.

Water flows downward through something that should not physically exist.

The squid crosses the gate.

The player sees the Pacific Ocean, Earth, stars and cosmic waters layered over one another.

Vishnu and Shiva are no longer distant mythological images.

Their conflict becomes environmental reality.

Preservation is freezing entire worlds into permanence.

Dissolution is tearing entire timelines apart.

The Asuric mechanism is forcing both principles into catastrophe.

The player must enter the mechanism itself.

---

# 13. BOSSES SHOULD BE ENVIRONMENTS

Avoid generic health-bar monsters whenever possible.

Major encounters should mix traversal, ecology and combat.

Examples:

### The Sleeper Whale

A colossal whale infected by Asuric organisms.

The player swims across and inside its body while attempting to remove parasites without killing it.

### Nāga of the Thermocline

An enormous serpent moving through vertical currents.

The squid must grapple sections of its body and travel with it through the ocean.

### The Drowned Rig

An abandoned human deep-sea installation possessed by an organism that has grown through the entire structure.

The building itself behaves like a creature.

### The Blind Leviathan

Cannot see chromatophores.

It detects pressure disturbances.

Jet propulsion becomes extremely dangerous.

The safest strategy is tentacle crawling along trench walls.

### Asuric Makara

A gigantic biomechanical marine guardian.

Its armor must be opened by physically grappling several points at once.

### Tandava

Not literally a boss fight against Shiva.

The environment itself begins collapsing rhythmically.

The player must navigate destruction synchronized with a cosmic percussion pattern.

---

# 14. PROCEDURAL WORLD GENERATION

Do not create a completely random ocean.

Use **procedural curation**.

Every biome contains:

1. A authored macro route downward.
2. Procedural cavern and side-route generation.
3. Procedural ecology.
4. Procedural geological decoration.
5. Rare hand-authored master locations.

The world therefore remains coherent while still feeling different between incarnations.

## Master locations

Create a library of highly polished authored locations that can appear inside procedural regions.

Examples:

* Whale graveyard
* Sunken submarine
* Nāga temple
* Brine waterfall
* Giant clam cavern
* Crystal forest
* Asuric machine room
* Hydrothermal cathedral
* Vertical shark migration shaft
* Abandoned research station
* Underwater ossuary
* Black smoker labyrinth

Once encountered during an incarnation, the same master room should not repeat.

---

# 15. PROCEDURAL ECOLOGY

Creatures should not simply spawn randomly around the player.

Each species gets ecological rules:

* Preferred depth
* Temperature
* Light level
* Terrain
* Food
* Predator relationships
* Schooling behavior
* Migration
* Territory
* Fear
* Curiosity
* Attraction to light
* Attraction to blood
* Attraction to vibrations

Schools should behave like actual collective organisms.

Predators should hunt prey even when the player is absent.

The player should be able to exploit ecological relationships.

Examples:

Ink attracts one species while repelling another.

Injuring prey may attract sharks.

Turning off bioluminescence may hide the player from visual hunters but make sonar hunters more dangerous.

Following dolphins may lead to feeding grounds.

Whale falls create temporary ecosystems.

---

The game needs wonder as much as danger.

Include:

* Riding turtles
* Swimming beside whales
* Playing with dolphins
* Following migrating manta rays
* Cleaning parasites from animals
* Restoring damaged coral
* Discovering rare species
* Finding hidden caves
* Communicating through color
* Solving ecological problems
* Activating ancient sanctuaries
* Escorting juvenile creatures
* Observing hunting behavior
* Discovering human wrecks
* Collecting memories rather than physical collectibles

Create an internal natural-history codex.

When the player discovers a new real species, show its name and basic information subtly.

The ocean itself becomes part game and part virtual aquarium.

# 17. WATER VISUALS

The ocean should be one of the most beautiful water environments possible in a browser.

Above and near the surface implement:

* Dynamic waves
* Foam
* Refraction
* Reflection
* Sun glitter
* Shoreline interaction
* Underwater caustics

Below water implement:

* Wavelength-dependent light absorption
* Volumetric scattering
* Depth-dependent color
* Suspended particles
* Marine snow
* God rays
* Local bioluminescent lighting
* Distortion from temperature gradients
* Hydrothermal shimmer
* Ink volume rendering

Red light should disappear rapidly.

The upper ocean transitions from turquoise to blue.

The deep ocean becomes increasingly monochromatic until biological lights provide most visible color.

At mythological depths, impossible colors gradually return.

# 18. GRAPHICAL STYLE

Aim for realistic marine biology rather than cartoon sea creatures.

The surrealism comes from composition, lighting, scale and eventually impossible organisms.

The squid itself must be beautiful enough to watch for hours.

Implement:

* Translucent skin
* Subsurface appearance
* Iridescence
* Dynamic chromatophores
* Wet specular response
* Visible eyes
* Responsive pupils
* Mantle deformation
* Breathing
* Fin undulation

* Sucker detail

Animals should be recognizable real species whenever possible.

Mythological creatures should appear as plausible evolutionary forms first and supernatural beings second.

# 19. VEDIC VISUAL LANGUAGE

Avoid simply covering every wall with random religious symbols.

The spiritual imagery should evolve gradually.

Early game:

Natural spirals, symmetry and subtle repeating geometry.

Mid game:

Lotus geometry, serpent forms, yantra-like arrangements and architecture that resembles temples without literally being terrestrial temples.

Deep game:

Reality itself develops mandala structures.

Schools of fish arrange themselves geometrically.

Hydrothermal currents form rotating patterns.

Chromatophore pulses resemble Sanskrit-like geometric language.

The Sudarshana Chakra can be interpreted as enormous rotating structures or vortices.

Shiva's trident can appear geometrically in branching trench formations.

Shesha can appear both as a divine serpent and as a recurring structural pattern.

# 21. CAMERA

Use a cinematic third-person aquatic camera.

The camera should lag slightly behind turns to communicate fluid motion without creating input latency.

Dynamic distance:

* Close when crawling through caves
* Medium during normal exploration
* Far during high-speed swimming
* Very far when controlling the late-game giant squid

When gigantic creatures appear, subtly reframe the camera to emphasize their scale.

Never prevent the player from controlling the animal merely for cinematic effect unless it is an intentional cutscene.

# 22. CONTROLS

Desktop:

* WASD: directional swimming
* Mouse: aim/orientation
* Shift: mantle jet
* Space: ascend
* Ctrl/C: descend
* Left mouse: primary tentacle interaction
* Right mouse: multi-arm grapple
* Q: ink
* E: sonar
* R: chromatophore mode
* F: contextual interaction

Controller should be first-class.

Analog sticks are ideal for swimming.

Add optional advanced tentacle controls but do not require the player to manually manipulate eight arms simultaneously.

# 24. WEBGPU

Use WebGPU for systems that benefit significantly from parallel computation.

Good candidates:

* Fish schooling
* Marine snow
* Plankton
* Ink particles
* Large creature populations
* Current fields
* Procedural vegetation movement
* Bioluminescent particle fields
* Certain procedural terrain operations

Use Three.js TSL/node materials wherever appropriate.

Provide WebGL2 fallback wherever practical.

---

# 25. PHYSICS

Use a hybrid system.

Rigid objects and major collisions can use Rapier or equivalent.

Do not attempt to simulate every tentacle section as a full rigidbody chain.

Tentacles should use a specialized position-based or Verlet-style simulation combined with IK and collision queries.

Underwater movement requires custom forces:

* Drag
* Angular drag
* Buoyancy
* Current force
* Mantle thrust
* Fin thrust
* Tentacle impulses

Creatures should feel heavy underwater while remaining responsive.

---

# 26. STREAMING

Never attempt to keep the entire Mariana descent in memory.

Create vertical world cells.

Stream cells according to:

* Player depth
* Horizontal position
* Camera direction
* Velocity

Keep distant terrain at aggressively simplified LOD.

Use instancing for:

* Coral
* Sponges
* Rocks
* Plants
* Small fish
* Vent worms
* Particles

Large recognizable animals get full animated models.

# 28. PERFORMANCE TARGET

The game must remain playable in a browser despite high visual ambition.

Build quality tiers.

### Ultra

Full WebGPU effects, high creature populations, volumetrics and high-density vegetation.

### High

Slightly reduced particles and population.

### Medium

Reduced simulation distance, vegetation and volumetric quality.

### Low

Aggressive LOD and reduced post-processing while maintaining art direction.

Target 60 FPS on capable modern hardware.

Never allow one beautiful effect to destroy the whole game's performance.

---
